# Medication Formulary and Coverage Policy

Riverbend Health Network (fictional) — Pharmacy Policy RX-120. Effective January 1, 2026.

## Formulary tiers
Payer formularies in this network use five tiers. Tier 1 is preferred generics with the lowest copay. Tier 2 is non-preferred generics and preferred brands. Tier 3 is non-preferred brands. Tier 4 is high-cost drugs with coinsurance. Tier 5 is specialty drugs, which are usually dispensed by a specialty pharmacy and require prior authorization.

## Step therapy
Some plans require a trial of a lower-cost drug before covering a higher-cost one. For GLP-1 receptor agonists such as semaglutide, commercial plans require a documented 90-day trial of metformin unless metformin is contraindicated. Medicaid requires metformin plus one other oral agent. Medicare Advantage covers GLP-1 drugs only with a type 2 diabetes diagnosis; they are not covered for weight loss alone.

## Formulary exceptions
When a drug is not on a patient's plan formulary, the prescriber may file a formulary exception request with a statement that the formulary alternatives would be less effective or would cause harm. Standard exception requests are decided within 72 hours; expedited requests within 24 hours.

## Generic substitution
Pharmacists substitute AB-rated generics automatically unless the prescriber writes "dispense as written" with a clinical reason.

## Anticoagulants
Direct oral anticoagulants such as apixaban are preferred over warfarin for non-valvular atrial fibrillation on commercial and Medicare plans. Medicaid requires a warfarin trial unless contraindicated. Patients on any anticoagulant should avoid routine NSAID use.

## Controlled substances
Opioid prescriptions such as tramadol are limited to a 30-day supply. Prescribers must check the state prescription monitoring program before prescribing and every 90 days thereafter.
